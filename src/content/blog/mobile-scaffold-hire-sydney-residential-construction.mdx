## What Should You Consider Before Hiring Mobile Scaffolding?

Before arranging mobile scaffold hire, contractors should consider several important factors.

### 1. Working Height

Identify the height at which the work needs to be completed.

Discuss the required working height with your scaffold provider so the appropriate equipment can be considered.

Do not confuse platform height with working height. Refer to the equipment manufacturer's specifications.

### 2. Floor and Ground Conditions

Mobile scaffolding requires suitable supporting conditions.

Consider whether the equipment will be used on a level, stable surface and whether there are any floor obstructions, slopes or changes in level.

The manufacturer's requirements for the supporting surface must be followed.

### 3. Available Working Space

Check the width of access areas, doorways, hallways and other restrictions.

The scaffold must be suitable for the available space and the intended work area.

### 4. Intended Work Activities

Explain whether the scaffold will be used for painting, electrical work, ceiling maintenance or another task.

Different jobs may have different access and equipment requirements.

### 5. Equipment Specifications

Ask about the equipment's intended use, platform configuration, working limitations and manufacturer's instructions.

Do not assume that all mobile scaffolds have identical specifications.

### 6. Movement and Positioning

Discuss how the equipment should be positioned and whether it needs to be moved between work locations.

Follow the manufacturer's instructions and relevant safety requirements.

Never assume that a mobile scaffold can be moved while occupied.

### 7. Delivery and Hire Arrangements

Before confirming your hire, discuss equipment availability, delivery arrangements, collection and the expected hire period.

Make sure these details are understood before the project begins.

## Mobile Scaffold Safety Considerations

Mobile scaffolding should be selected, assembled, inspected and used in accordance with its design, manufacturer's instructions and applicable workplace safety requirements.

Some important considerations include:

- Check that the equipment is suitable for the intended task.
- Ensure the supporting surface is appropriate.
- Follow the manufacturer's assembly instructions.
- Check that guardrails and other required protective components are correctly installed.
- Do not exceed the equipment's specified capacity.
- Do not use damaged or unsuitable equipment.
- Consider overhead electrical services and other hazards.
- Follow instructions relating to castors, brakes and stabilising components.
- Do not move an occupied scaffold unless the equipment is specifically designed and approved for that method.
- Ensure workers understand the equipment's safe-use requirements.

The specific safety requirements depend on the equipment, work activity and site conditions.

Contractors should assess the risks and obtain appropriate advice where required.

## When Is Fixed Scaffolding a Better Option?

Although mobile scaffolding can be suitable for certain tasks, it is not the right solution for every residential construction project.

Fixed scaffolding may be more appropriate when:

- Work is required around several external sections of a house.
- Roof access is needed for repairs or replacement.
- A larger working platform is required.
- The project involves multiple building levels.
- The site layout does not provide suitable conditions for mobile equipment.
- The work requires a more extensive access arrangement.

For larger residential construction projects, builders should discuss their requirements with a scaffolding provider before deciding on the appropriate arrangement.
